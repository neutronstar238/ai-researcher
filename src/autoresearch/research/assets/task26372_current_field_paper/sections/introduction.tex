\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Research automation is no longer represented by one generic agent loop.
Peer-reviewed systems now automate substantial but distinct parts of the
research process. The AI Scientist joins ideation, implementation,
experimentation, manuscript production, and automated review in machine
learning~\cite{lu2026aiscientist}. Co-Scientist organizes generation,
critique, ranking, and evolution of hypotheses around expert objectives and
experimental feedback~\cite{gottweis2026coscientist}. Robin connects
literature-grounded hypotheses with human-run biology experiments and
machine-assisted analysis~\cite{ghareeb2026robin}. ERA searches over
empirical software under machine-scored objectives while explicitly
separating score optimization from scientific discovery~\cite{aygun2026era}.
These systems make breadth of automation an unsuitable novelty claim by
itself.

Evaluation evidence is more guarded. The peer-reviewed AstaBench study spans
many agents and scientific-assistance tasks and finds that current systems do
not solve the suite as a whole~\cite{bragg2026astabench}. The peer-reviewed
REPRO-Bench study reports low accuracy when agents assess realistic social
science reproduction packages~\cite{hu2025reprobench}. Two public preprint
benchmarks, CORE-Bench and PaperBench, also report low performance on
computational reproduction and paper-scale replication
tasks~\cite{siegel2024corebench,starace2025paperbench}. These results do not
measure original discovery directly, but they show why a system's own local
checks cannot stand in for independent evaluation.

This paper asks a more limited question. When a computational experiment
fails, can a research controller preserve the decision, connect a successor
to that failure, freeze the successor's evaluation before inspecting its
outcome, and prevent unsupported report claims? This is a question about a
research state machine, an experimental harness, and an evidence graph. It is
not a test of whether the controller produces important discoveries or
performs better than current research systems.

Figure~\ref{fig:running_example} illustrates the unit of mechanism studied.
A retry may change code or prose without creating a new scientific round. A
counted successor must cite a parent result, state a mechanism change, commit
its falsification rule, run against the frozen contract, and retain the
decision regardless of sign.

The implementation, \method{}, stores campaign state in a versioned,
Obsidian-compatible \vault{}. Structured artifacts record hypotheses,
preregistrations, code and data hashes, run manifests, raw metrics,
validation decisions, failure analyses, claim-evidence edges, and manuscript
versions. A language model may propose structured text, while deterministic
code controls partitions, metrics, thresholds, state transitions, and
acceptance decisions. Hashes make replacement detectable; they do not certify
scientific merit.

The empirical record has two parts. First, two parent-linked
equation-discovery candidates pass development screens and fail their
respective unseen criteria. The negative results remain in the lineage and
cause a registered route change. Second, a frozen matrix injects controlled
workflow faults into four fresh UCI executions and six previously observed
MDBench traces. Three controller modes and four ablations exercise the state
machine. The three recorded repetitions per task are deterministic
idempotency checks rather than independent samples.

The first contribution is an executable definition of a counted research
successor with parent-result linkage, pre-outcome freezing, and visible
negative decisions. The second is a claim-evidence boundary that separates a
scientific-method gate, a controlled workflow gate, and a manuscript audit.
The third is an additive correction that treats the ten tasks, rather than 30
duplicated task-repetition cells, as the independent units. This correction
preserves the observed mean difference of \TaskLevelGain{} while widening the
task bootstrap interval to [\TaskLevelCILower{}, \TaskLevelCIUpper{}] and
giving a two-sided sign-test \(p\)-value of \SignTestTwoSided{}.

The intended conclusion is correspondingly narrow. The frozen study supports
a controlled demonstration of enforcement and traceability. It does not
establish performance on independently authored research tasks, stochastic
controller reliability, broad scientific autonomy, or readiness for external
submission. Those questions require a new protocol and human-owned decisions.
